## ACL Special Interest Group on Similar Languages, Varieties, and Dialects

SIGVAR was funded in September 2026.

### Mission

Variation is intrinsic to human language. It is manifested at different dimensions such as diachronic, diastratic, and diatopic. With over 7,000 languages spoken in the world, most of them with multiple dialects and regional varieties, diatopic variation is arguably the most important dimension to be considered when developing NLP applications. Furthermore, considering dialects which often do not have a written standard presents its own particular set of challenges.

The purpose of the [Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL)](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/) Special Interest Group on Similar Languages, Varieties, and Dialects (SIGVAR) is to promote interest in all aspects of diatopic language variation and multilingualism and to foster collaboration between researchers in NLP/CL and in variationist linguistics. 

The activities of SIGVAR include:
- The exchange and propagation of research results, relevant to language varieties and dialects.
- Coordination between organizations in academia (both in NLP and in dialectology and linguistics) and industry on this topic.
- Fostering open-source language resource creation, maintenance, and release.
- Endorsing the VarDial workshop and other relevant events. 

### Statement of Purpose

Estimates point to over 7,000 languages spoken in the world. Only a small fraction of these languages is well-represented in NLP research with foundational models, corpora, and resources. Many of these languages have multiple national language varieties and dialects, often without a written standard. 
The purpose of the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL) Special Interest Group on Similar Languages, Varieties, and Dialects (SIGVAR)  is to promote interest in all aspects of language variation and multilingualism, especially diatopic variation. More specifically, SIGVAR aims to promote research on the processing of low-resource languages, varieties, and dialects, including but not limited to morphological analysis and generation, orthographic normalization, syntactic parsing, corpus annotation and benchmark creation, semantic and pragmatic analysis, dialectometry, optical character recognition, machine translation, information retrieval, information extraction, text analytics, language modelling, and speech processing. SIGVAR also aims to promote coordination between organizations in academia and industry throughout the world performing research on computational approaches related to language variation, whether they come from computational linguistics, natural language processing, speech processing, dialectology, or variationist linguistics. Furthermore, SIGVAR aims to provide ACL members who are interested in low-resource languages, language varieties, and dialects with a means of exchanging news of recent research developments and other matters of interest. Finally, SIGVAR will sponsor regular, timely and worthwhile meetings and workshops on this topic, such as the VarDial workshop. 
SIGVAR will operate within the framework of the ACL's general guidelines for SIGs.

### Ethical Considerations

SIGVAR supports research that extends useful technologies to more language varieties and speakers than are currently served by language technologies. SIGVAR also seeks to foster the development and release of open-source datasets.
SIGVAR recognizes potential ethical and dual-use issues in the context of processing low-resource language data (e.g., technologies generating non-standard dialects can be perceived as parodying the dialect, and people might use non-standard dialects in order for their language not to be understood by technologies). SIGVAR aims to raise awareness of such issues (e.g., in the calls for papers for associated workshops) and encourages research and resource development that centers the wants and needs of the relevant speaker communities.
